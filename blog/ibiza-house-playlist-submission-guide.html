How to get into Ibiza House playlists on Spotify

22 May 2026 · 7 min read · By Flotion Records

Ibiza House isn't a Spotify genre tag. It's a vibe defined by where the music plays: sunset bars in San Antonio, beach clubs in Playa d'en Bossa, terraces in Ses Salines. If you want your track on an Ibiza House playlist, you have to make a track that fits those rooms.

What "Ibiza House" actually sounds like

Ibiza House overlaps with Deep House, Organic House, Melodic House and Afro House but isn't quite any of them. The defining elements you'll hear in 80% of tracks on top Ibiza House playlists:

  • BPM: 118-124. Sweet spot 120-122. Anything above 125 feels too club-floor. Below 118 feels too lounge.
  • Groove: four-on-the-floor kick with off-beat hi-hats, but lighter than mainstream House. Often live percussion (congas, shakers, tambourine) layered in.
  • Melodic content: piano stabs, Rhodes chords, plucked guitars, vintage analog leads. Often a recognisable hook that develops over 8 bars.
  • Vocal style: when present, soulful and warm. Female vocals are common but the genre also leans into Latin/Spanish vocal phrases, distant chants, or vocal chops used melodically rather than lyrically.
  • Atmosphere: reverb-heavy, wide stereo image, golden-hour energy. Listeners often have this on at sunset, at beach clubs, while driving the coast road. Make it sound like that.

Reference tracks worth studying

Pull up any of these and study the mix, the arrangement, the moment the drop lands. We're not telling you to copy them — we're telling you to internalise the sonic language:

  • Anyma & Cassian - Eternity
  • Argy & Omnya - Aeon
  • Solomun - any recent Diynamic release
  • Black Coffee feat. Pharrell Williams - 10 Missed Calls
  • Adriatique - X

Production decisions that matter

The intro problem

Club tracks classically have 16-32 bar mix-in intros. Playlist tracks need to land the vibe within 15 seconds because that's when listeners decide to skip. The solve: keep the intro short (8-16 bars) and make sure the atmosphere — pads, that Ibiza guitar, the rhythmic groove — is established immediately. The first kick can wait until bar 9, but the feeling can't.

The drop without the drop

Ibiza House rarely has the kind of EDM-style buildup-and-drop that dominates festival House. The energy peaks are subtler: a vocal phrase coming in, a melody layering, the bass sliding from sub-only into the mid-range. If your track has a 20-second filter-sweep followed by a kick-drop, you're making festival House, not Ibiza House. Curators will skip.

Mix targets

  • Integrated loudness: -10 to -8 LUFS. Hotter than typical Spotify normalisation target (-14) because the genre rewards energy without being smashed. Spotify will turn you down to -14 either way, so make sure the peaks are clean.
  • True peak: -1.0 dBTP minimum headroom. -1.5 is safer if your track has aggressive limiting.
  • Low end: kick and bass mono below 120 Hz. Stereo low end becomes phase mush on club sound systems and on phone speakers alike.
  • High end: don't fear it. Ibiza House lives in the 8-15 kHz air range. The bright shimmer of percussion and pad reverb is part of the genre signature.

Submission strategy

Three weeks before release

Pitch to Spotify editorial via Spotify for Artists. Fill in mood (Chill, Romantic, Energetic), instruments (Synthesizers, Piano, Drums), genre (House, Deep House), and the vocal style fields. Be honest. Editorial cross-references and a misleading pitch flags your account.

One week before release

Submit to independent curators. For paid submission services, pick ones that explicitly curate the Ibiza/Beach/Sunset niche — submitting to a generic "House Curator" probably means a Berlin techno enthusiast who'll reject your sunset chord progression for being too soft. We curate specifically Ibiza House.

Release week

Drive traffic from your own channels. Instagram reels with a 15-second hook clip. TikTok with a sunset visual. Email your list. The algorithm uses week-one engagement to decide whether to push you in Release Radar and Discover Weekly. Without organic week-one signal, even an editorial placement won't grow.

Three to six weeks after release

This is when Discover Weekly placements compound. If your week-one signal was strong, the algorithm starts adding the track to listener Discover Weekly lists in similar-taste profiles. Streams now come from people who've never heard of you. That's the real growth phase.

What disqualifies your track for Ibiza House playlists

  • BPM outside 118-124 (118-122 ideal)
  • Distorted, harsh or over-compressed master
  • Aggressive synth leads typical of Big Room or Festival House
  • Lo-fi or muddy production — the genre demands a polished mix
  • No melodic hook — pure groove tracks fit Tech House, not Ibiza House
  • Vocals in the wrong language for the mood (English/Spanish/wordless work; aggressive rap vocals don't)
